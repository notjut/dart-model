The numbers behind the matchup

Current season through the latest checked inputs. “Successful plays” gain enough yards to keep a drive on track. For defensive success allowed, lower is better. Rankings compare the teams in the loaded upcoming schedule.

What do these stats mean?

Team strength rating: a rating based on previous wins and losses, with more credit for beating stronger opponents. Higher means stronger. This is also called Elo.

Successful play: gains at least half the yards needed on first down, 70% on second down, or enough for a first down on third or fourth down.

Successful-play advantage: your offense’s successful-play rate minus the rate your defense allows. A positive number is favorable. “pp” means percentage points: 45% minus 40% equals +5 percentage points.

Big-play rate: the share of counted offensive plays gaining at least 20 yards. Small samples are blended toward a common baseline so one game does not dominate the rating.

Play-level definitions

Missing play-by-play is excluded from rate denominators, not counted as failed plays. Incomplete game coverage and excluded plays mean these are our calculated rates, not an official provider’s success-rate measure. No garbage-time filter or opponent adjustment is applied to the displayed rates.

Training and testing

Two standardized ridge regressions predict home margin and combined points (fixed penalty 30). The core model uses Elo, net success rate, scoring margin, scoring environment, home field, explosive rate and yards. The context model adds home/road residuals, rest, post-win, look-ahead, evening and series effects. Team-specific residuals are heavily shrunk. Rankings are descriptive and are not added again as separate predictors.

Pregame snapshots use only results from earlier calendar dates. Training begins with 100 eligible games; each later date is held out together. Eligibility requires at least three prior games and 80 eligible offensive plays for each team. Model weights are then refit on all eligible completed games for upcoming forecasts. Source corrections and retrospective schedule availability mean this is a reconstructed backtest, not a fully archived point-in-time production trial.

Win and 21+ margin probabilities use a normal distribution of margin error. Held-out intervals use each training fit’s residual standard deviation (floor 10 points); upcoming forecasts use held-out error spread when enough observations exist. These intervals do not include all parameter, injury or data uncertainty. A normal approximation misses some football scoring structure.

Matchup oddities

Team streaks, last wins, one-score finishes, venue records and extreme results are calculated from loaded 2016–2026 regular-season schedules. They are not all-time claims; postseason completeness is not guaranteed. Coach trivia is sourced from official reporting. Player facts match athlete IDs in current 2026 rosters to prior 2023–26 matchup box scores, with explicit sample counts. These are descriptive performances, not proof a player performs better against that opponent than against other schools. Facts do not add points to the forecast.

Upset meter & weekly shortlist

The meter shows the model’s win probability for the market underdog. Missing spreads and pick’em games have no market underdog and no upset percentage. Risk labels are editorial: below 20% lower, 20–34% live possibility, 35–50% elevated, above 50% model favors the upset. The top three games in your filters are ranked by number of different warning-sign types, then win probability, and require a posted nonzero market spread. This rule is not itself backtested.

Sportsbook spread and model spread are separate. A negative number means that team is favored by that many points. Provider odds are retrieved snapshots, not live prices or bookmaker-update timestamps. TV listings can change, and unconfirmed kickoff times display TBA.

The “perfect storm” standard

We group warning signs into independent evidence families: competitive baseline, efficiency, location/schedule, and recent series. Three aligned families trigger “Multiple upset signals.” This is a transparent explanation rule, not a validated storm classifier. It cannot certify a perfect storm while major injury or QB inputs are missing. Blowout probability is shown separately; high variance can increase both upset and blowout tails.

Data sources & next connections

ESPN schedules, results and game play-by-play supply this snapshot. CollegeFootballData authenticated API access is a possible next source for more historical advanced data. Historical regular-season schedules cover 2016–2026; postseason completeness is not guaranteed and missing team-seasons are disclosed. No API credential is configured here. The daily updater retrieves schedules, results and public availability reports. Numerical injury effects and a comprehensive historical motivation database are not yet fitted.
